# Architecture

How a story gets from a publisher's feed to the page, and what the reader does with it.

## The pipeline

Every hour the Build workflow runs [`build.py`](../build.py):

1. **Fetch:** reads every feed in [`feeds.opml`](../feeds.opml). A broken feed is skipped, never fatal.
2. **Filter:** keeps dated entries from the last few days that haven't been seen before, capped per feed.
3. **Score:** sends each new story to Claude Haiku (`claude-haiku-5-5`) on its own, through the Claude Code CLI on a Claude subscription, so a story's score never depends on what it was batched with. Each gets a category, a 1-10 relevance score judged within its category, a one-sentence summary, and whether it reports an exploited flaw (with the words that say so).
4. **Keep the notable ones:** a story is published if it clears its category's bar (8 for Security and Regulation, 7 for AI and Other, 6 for Infrastructure and Engineering) and isn't a repeat, or it reports an exploited flaw (below). There are no per-category quotas, so a big security day shows as one and a slow day stays short. Nothing published is ever taken down; a 20-story daily cap exists only to catch a malfunction, like a broken feed.
5. **Drop repeats:** the model flags a story that's already on the site from the past week (the same launch from another outlet, say). The code checks that claim, so one copy always survives.
6. **Flag exploited flaws:** a story is flagged if a CVE it names is in CISA's [Known Exploited Vulnerabilities](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) catalog, or it's a security story the model says reports an exploited flaw, a zero-day, an emergency patch or an active supply-chain compromise, and the words it quotes are in the story and speak of exploitation or attack. A flaw is **Exploited**, with its remedy in CISA's terms (patch, mitigation only, or no patch yet), from the scoring call and, for a published story, the summary call that reads the whole article, each checked against a quote; a CISA listing rules out "no patch yet" (the catalog only takes flaws with a remedy) without saying which, and a later story naming the same CVE with a patch or mitigation raises it. A flaw flagged after it was published (a later CISA listing) has its remedy read once from its upshot, and passed to other stories naming the same CVE. CVEs come from a story's title, excerpt, summary and upshot (which names the flaw's CVE when the article does), never the rest of the article, which cites older flaws for background; a repeat hands its CVEs to the copy on the site. The week before the flag shipped had the claim check run once on its security stories. RSS titles carry the label as a prefix ("Exploited: ", "Exploited, no patch yet: ", "Compromised: "), and a URL in `not-exploited.txt` is never flagged. A supply-chain compromise is **Compromised**, as GitHub and OSV keep malware apart from vulnerabilities, with no patch status: its steps are in the upshot. Only an exploited flaw with no patch yet (a zero-day) may publish a point under its category's bar, a repeat hands its flag to the copy on the site, later builds flag stories whose CVEs CISA lists afterwards, and each build's run summary counts the flagged and the below-the-bar ones. If the catalog can't be read, the build carries on with the checked claims alone.
7. **Pick the essentials:** once a day, the model chooses the week's five.
8. **Extract:** [`extract.mjs`](../extract.mjs) pulls each new story's text into Cloudflare KV for the reader (see below).
9. **Publish:** writes [`site/data.json`](../site/data.json) and [`site/rss.xml`](../site/rss.xml), remembers seen URLs in `state.json`, commits, and Cloudflare deploys the Worker on the push.

Everything older than 90 days is pruned, so the data stays small.

## The site

[`site/index.html`](../site/index.html) and [`site/app.js`](../site/app.js) are the whole front end: no framework, no build step.

The layout follows desktop feed readers such as Feedbin, in three widths:

- **Phones (up to 640px):** pages, as in Feedbin's app. Upshot opens on the topics; picking one opens its list, with a back chevron and its name in the header, and a story covers the list. Each page slides over the one before and follows the finger when swiped. Back, the chevron and a swipe right step back through them; a swipe left goes forward again, into the list just left or the story just closed. Articles read once are kept for the visit, so reopening one is instant.
- **641–1023px:** the reader docked on the right, as from 1024px, and the topics and the list as two pages of one left pane, scrolled sideways, as in Feedbin. Upshot opens on the list, and picking a topic slides to its list. The pane narrows (to 280px at 641px) so the article keeps at least 360px.
- **1024px and up:** sidebar, list and reader side by side. Dragging the sidebar's or the list's edge resizes it; the widths are kept in the browser, and a double-click resets them.

From 641px there is no full-width header: the logo tops the left pane, and the menu sits at the end of the reader bar. The reader stays docked, the list scrolls on its own, and it stays live while an article is open, so `j`/`k` and clicks move through it.

The essentials are an entry in the topics, not a separate section, so no story is listed twice.

Lists run newest first, by the age each row shows; stories of the same age keep the build's order, most relevant first. A row carries at most one label, the one that matters most, the same in every list: **Exploited** and its remedy: "No patch yet" red with the word, the only filled mark on a row, while there's nothing to apply; "Patch available" in the accent; "Mitigation only" with no colour; nothing when the story doesn't say; a supply-chain attack says **Compromised** in yellow, a warning rather than a danger; otherwise **Essential**, which the Essentials list leaves off its own rows. Unread titles are bold, so nothing marks new stories. The last seven days' exploited flaws are listed in their own entry under the essentials, which shows only when there are any, with "N unpatched" in place of its unread count while any has no fix; the reader shows the same label, and "confirmed by CISA" when the catalog lists it. The sidebar counts unread stories, as a feed reader does. A first visit starts with the last day's stories unread, or the newest five after a quiet stretch, and older ones show as read; after that, anything added since counts. The week's essentials count as unread until opened, however old. An older story marked unread by hand stays unread. A toolbar after Feedbin's holds two buttons, each centred in half of it: at the sidebar's foot from 1024px (the pane's below that), **Shortcuts** and **Read**; on a phone, a list's bottom bar with **Top** (back to the top, dimmed when there) and **Read**. Read marks the list that's showing read once confirmed (`Shift A` too), and the `…` on the selected story (the one open, or on a phone the one just closed) marks the stories above or below it. Bulk marks go in at the oldest end of the read list, so the search panel's "Recently read" still starts with what was opened.

Each time Upshot opens, or comes back after 15 minutes away (but not on a refresh or a new tab within 15 minutes of the last pass), the wordmark shows for a moment, then its spot runs once through the date, when the news last updated and when the next build is due (from the build's schedule, `17 * * * *` UTC, and left out when a build is late), then settles back on the wordmark, after Kagi News. The sidebar's foot keeps saying it after that, "Updated 1h ago" just above the toolbar (on a phone, a thin strip over a list's bottom bar), and gives way to the late bar, in the same place, when a build is late. The pass takes under five seconds, so it needs no pause control under WCAG 2.2.2, and a tap ends it. [`site/_headers`](../site/_headers) sets the Content-Security-Policy. [`worker.js`](../worker.js) serves the static files and answers `/api/read` for the reader, and on a cron trigger starts the Build workflow, since GitHub's scheduler runs late or skips slots; [`wrangler.jsonc`](../wrangler.jsonc) configures the Worker.

## The reader, and what it does with your data

Each build extracts new stories with Mozilla's [Readability](https://github.com/mozilla/readability), the same approach as Firefox's reader view, and stores the result in Cloudflare KV. Opening an article calls `/api/read`, which the Worker answers with what's stored. Parsing happens in the build because it costs far more CPU than the Worker is allowed per request on the free plan.

Articles are set in Mercury Text, with their headline in Mercury Display, its cut for large sizes; everything else, from the upshot to the story list, is in Söhne. The split follows whose words they are: the publisher's in Mercury, Upshot's own in Söhne. All three are licensed, so they can't sit in this public repo: they live in a private R2 bucket, `upshot-fonts`, and the Worker serves them on the site's own origin under `/fonts/licensed/`. Those paths have no extension so the Worker runs for them, and the Worker answers only for `.woff2` files, with a CORS header that admits the reader frame but not other sites. Mercury Text comes in grades that differ only in darkness, at identical widths: the reader uses G2 in the light theme and the lighter G1 in the dark, where light text on a dark ground reads heavier, and switching themes never reflows a line. Mercury's x-height is small (0.448 of the em), so its faces carry `size-adjust: 113%`. Söhne replaced Public Sans, and the weights the CSS asks for were tuned for Public Sans, so each Söhne face takes the range of weights whose rendered ink it matches (370 and 500 Buch, 600 Kräftig, 700 Halbfett), and `size-adjust: 108%` sets it as wide as Public Sans was, so lines break and clamp where they did. H&Co's Mercury files are cleaned before upload: Mercury Text's CFF hints are removed, because Firefox's font sanitizer rejected them, and in both Text and Display the ffi and ffl ligatures that sat at U+2248 and U+221A are dropped, so ≈ and √ fall back to a font that has them. Without the bucket, as in a fork, the site uses the system sans and serif. Public Sans stays in `fonts/`, outside the site, for the one thing the build draws itself: `og.png`, the link preview.

The reader is a sandboxed frame, which the browser caches apart each time, so fonts loaded there by URL were downloaded again for every article. Instead, the page fetches each font once in the background after the list loads, cached as usual, and hands it to every frame inline as a `data:` URL; the page's CSP allows `data:` fonts for this.

- **Each article opens with the upshot.** Two to four bullets, written once by the build from the article itself. Every figure in a bullet (CVE IDs, versions, dates, counts) must appear word for word in the article, or that bullet is dropped. The weekly essentials add their hand-picked "What to do" as the last line.
- **Page furniture is removed by what it is, never by who published it.** Bylines, dates, tables of contents, same-page links and "N min read" lines come out before the article is stored. If that would remove more than 35% of the text, the article is kept whole instead, because a stray byline beats a lost paragraph.
- **Article text is stored, but not in this repository.** It sits in a private KV namespace and expires after 100 days, shortly after a story leaves the site. It's a snapshot from when the story was first read, so later corrections by the publisher won't appear.
- **It says why when it can't.** Every response carries a reason: `ok`, `challenge` (the publisher blocks it), `shell`, `short`, `not_html` or `pending` (no article yet). The page already shows the story's summary from `data.json`, so a block never leaves it empty. Blocked stories are tried again on every build, and if a blocked publisher's feed carries whole articles, the build takes the article from the feed instead.
- **It isn't a proxy.** The Worker never fetches anything, and only returns articles the build stored.
- **The page can't run anything from the source site.** Extracted HTML is stripped of scripts, frames, styles and event handlers during the build, then rendered in a sandboxed frame with no access to the page. The only script allowed in there is the site's own swipe and keyboard relay, admitted by a per-render nonce.
- **A strict Content-Security-Policy backs all of this up.** Scripts load only from the site itself, plus two inline scripts allowed by exact hash; no plugins, forms or base-URL changes; network requests go only to the site. A test keeps the hashes in step with the code.
- **No login, no tracking.** What you've read lives in your browser's local storage and never leaves it.

## The Worker

[`wrangler.jsonc`](../wrangler.jsonc) holds the Worker's config: static assets from `site/` with `404.html` for unknown paths; code runs only for `/api/read` and for pages, which get a per-request CSP nonce so Cloudflare's Bot Fight Mode script can run; the `ARTICLES` KV binding; the custom domain; preview URLs for PRs; and Workers Logs, which keep a few days of the Worker's requests and errors.

Cloudflare Workers Builds deploys every push to `main` with `npx wrangler deploy`, including the bot's data commits, and builds a Preview for every other branch with its URL posted on the PR.

## It looks after itself

- A feed that has failed for two weeks, gone quiet or moved lands in a single "Feed health" issue that closes itself once it's fixed. For failing and quiet feeds it suggests any feeds the publisher's homepage advertises.
- A run where every LLM call fails exits with an error, so GitHub emails you.
- If Cloudflare KV is down, the stories are still published (the reader shows a fallback until a later build stores their text) and the run is marked failed after committing, so you hear about it without losing the build.
- When the data is more than 105 minutes old (a build due every hour is running at least 45 minutes late), a bar at the foot of the list says when it last updated, with a "Check again" button that fetches the data at once. Failed runs already email the repo's owner, so the bar is for readers and doesn't link to the build.
